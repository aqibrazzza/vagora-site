01 · Brand foundation

What Vagora is,
and only that.

The product noun is the Vagora Mirror. The software is the console. A retailer's mirrors are the estate. The site evidences ten capabilities and no others; nothing may be added to the list without evidence.

EVIDENCED CAPABILITIES Established
  1. 01Live 3D try-on — the garment rendered on the customer, tracking their movement in real time.
  2. 02The whole catalogue — every size and colourway, checked against live stock before it is shown.
  3. 03The whole look — pieces combined on the customer before anything is fetched.
  4. 04Made-to-order — unstitched cloth and made-to-order garments rendered before production begins.
  5. 05Campaign scenes — the customer placed inside scenes built around the collection.
  6. 06Their picks at the till — one scan sends selected garments, colourways and sizes to phone and counter.
  7. 07Retail intelligence — what customers browsed, tried, combined, saved and ignored, by store and garment.
  8. 08One console — catalogue, content and configuration across every store; sync status and offline alerts.
  9. 09Privacy — camera active only during a session; nothing stored; no account, registration or face database.
  10. 10Hardware — freestanding; starts by itself; 49-inch portrait display readable from a natural standing distance.
PERSONALITY Derived

Precise, calm, editorial, retail-literate, quietly confident. Vagora describes what happens rather than what it means. It does not sell; it shows and explains. Three tones are always present at once — remove any one and it stops being Vagora:

  1. FFashion — large Switzer, generous whitespace, full-bleed photography with strong colour from garments. Without it: SaaS.
  2. RRetail — till, catalogue, colourway, garment, rail, stock; the constant return to the floor and the counter. Without it: an art project.
  3. SSystem — Fragment Mono notation, numbered sequences, hairlines, the console object. Without it: a lookbook.
THE SPINE Established

Act one · On the floor → The Vagora Mirror → Act two · In the business → The console → Before you commit → See it in your store. Every piece of communication should be locatable on that spine.

HONESTY AS VOICE Established

“We have no benchmark to compare you against yet, and will not invent one.” “Sample data, not a live estate.” Where evidence is missing, the copy says so.

THE LOGO SYSTEM — FOUR VERSIONS, EIGHT ARTWORKS Established

Four logo versions, each supplied for a light and a dark surface. Nothing else is a Vagora logo: if a version is not in assets/brand/logo/ it has not been approved, and it is not to be drawn, recoloured, outlined or assembled from parts. -on-dark and -on-light name the surface the artwork is for, not the colour of the artwork.

LIGHT AND DARK Established

Light surface → the -on-light artwork (#0F0F0F). Dark surface → the -on-dark artwork (white). Every version exists in both, so there is always a correct file: choose the right asset instead of recolouring one, and never add an outline, glow, shadow or plate to make the wrong one work. On a photograph, place a logo only where §04's four conditions hold, and use the version that keeps its contrast there.

CLEAR SPACE — FROM THE ARTWORK'S OWN GEOMETRY New recommendation

Measured from the piece itself so it scales with it. Nothing enters it — type, hairlines, the frame's edge, a retailer's logo. These numbers are proposed, not approved: the supplied artwork carries no clear-space specification.

PLACEMENT New recommendation

The logo lives in a margin, aligned to the grid's edge, once per surface — found, not featured. The wordmark, when used, is a dominant object: low and left as in the footer, or centred only in the one centred composition.

04 · Typography

Two voices,
never blended.

Switzer is the editorial voice: everything Vagora says and everything a person can act on. Fragment Mono is the notation voice: the system naming, indexing and recording. It is not “the small font” — it is a different speaker.

EDITORIAL VOICE Established

Switzer

Variable, 100–900 available; the site uses 400 for body, 500 for headings and medium on white, 450 on black (white blooms). Nothing is bold. Negative tracking and tight leading tighten with size.

WRAPPING AND MEASURE Established

Headings balance, with breaks authored by <br> where they matter. Prose pretty. Numbers tabular. Measures: reading 42rem · copy 30rem · annotation 34ch (280–320px wide) · manifesto 24ch · Mirror heading 21ch, lede 40ch · ask 10ch.

EYEBROW → HEADING Established

One site-wide gap (24px) corrected per tier — display lifts by 0.09em − 5px, lead 0.05em − 1.4px, chapter 0.045em − 1.2px — so the seen gap is equal at every scale. Sections never set their own.

OUTSIDE THE SYSTEM Established

The dock's type (15→16 at 500), the console's metric numerals (40→64, −.035em), the preloader tagline (sized to the viewport) and the footer wordmark (an SVG). Not templates; do not derive tiers from them.

NOT YET DEFINED Not yet defined

Italic (Switzer Italic is loaded, never used). Non-Latin type (Fragment Mono ships as a Latin subset). Where Switzer might ever go below 16px.

TEXT ON PHOTOGRAPHY Derived

The website keeps type beside its photographs, not on them — its choice, not a law. Type may sit directly on a photograph when all four hold: genuine negative space (a wall, a floor, sky — not the subject); dependable contrast from the picture itself, with no scrim; nothing that matters covered — never the customer, the garment, the Mirror, its screen or UI, staff; and integration with the composition, aligned to the picture's own edges and axes. When any fails, use A · image + solid field (a white or black margin beside or below the image carrying the type) or B · image + editorial annotation plate (solid white or black, compact, zero radius, no blur, translucency, gradient or shadow, attached to an edge or axis, sized to its content). Do not: glassmorphism, translucent panels, backdrop blur behind content, scrims, smoked overlays, gradients under text, rounded floating cards. Blur exists only in the dock and the film toggle, which are locked controls, never content surfaces. Specimens in §15.2.

A–J · THE RULES Established Derived
  1. ASubject. Customer + Mirror; the Mirror alone; service around the Mirror; a business surface. Never garments without the Mirror; never abstract technology.
  2. BEnvironment. Bright contemporary stores, white/pale-beige walls, window daylight, sparse rails, plants, pale floors, generous negative space. Seamless white or black studios.
  3. CThe person. One adult, unposed, looking at the Mirror, not at camera. Staff in plain dark clothing, doing something.
  4. DInteraction. Not a touch device. Approximately 1.5–2 metres from the Mirror, at a comfortable hands-free viewing distance, standing comfortably and observing; hands away from the glass, a raised hand at chest or shoulder height; the phone only for the QR scan. Never tapping, swiping, leaning in, or gesturing theatrically.
  5. EProduct protection. 49-inch portrait display in a matte-black frame with square architectural corners and real depth; substantial black cabinet with a pronounced sloped upper face and four casters (whether that face is a separate material or colour is not yet defined); a Microsoft Kinect v2 on a bracket at the viewer-right edge at mid-display height. No logo, no cables, no controls on the frame. See the annotated diagram below.
  6. E2The screen. Not a reflection — a live try-on. Physical outfit ≠ virtual outfit, always: the customer wears one real outfit and the screen shows the same customer in a visibly different one — a different garment type and/or a clearly different colour, a colourway comparison included — with 2–4 thumbnails at an edge and the selected one matching the render.

  9. HLight. Soft daylight; the screen's glow is the second light. Product: one soft key in a black studio.
  10. IColour. Neutral, bright, low-contrast grade; whites white, blacks black; colour from garments. The lime-green props are photographic, not brand.

You write for Vagora, an interactive mirror for physical fashion retail. Write in British
